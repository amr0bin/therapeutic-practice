# Reflective journal

*Private. Started 20 September 2026.*

**Anonymised at the point of writing, never later.** No names, no initials, no
identifying details, and no combination of details that identifies. A caller
described as "in their twenties" is not anonymised if the shift, the date and the
setting sit three columns away in `hours-log.csv`. Write each entry so that it
would be safe if it were read aloud.

**What this is for.** Two things, and they are different.

1. **The practice.** Supervised helping work produces material that has to go
   somewhere, and a person who does not write it down carries it instead. This is
   the profession's ordinary failure mode and it is why attrition is high.
2. **The record.** By 2030 this is four years of evidence that the work was done
   thoughtfully. It is never quoted in an application — but it is what makes the
   statement possible to write honestly, and what the eight versions of
   `the-question.md` are drawn from.

**What it is not.** It is not notebook material. Nothing from here is quoted,
paraphrased, composited or "based on" in the notebook, the Substack, the review,
a statement or a thesis. The governing question is answered from the literature,
from the room in general terms, and from your own thinking — never from anyone
who came to you.

**Cadence.** Within twenty-four hours of any shift. `house-rules.md` and
`the-floor.md` both make the reflective note part of the service rather than an
addition to it.

## A shape that works, when one is wanted

Not a form to fill in. Four prompts that tend to produce something usable.

- What happened, in one sentence, with nobody identifiable in it.
- What I did, and what I nearly did instead.
- What it asked of me — what I noticed in myself during and after.
- What I would want a supervisor to tell me.

The fourth one is the one that matters. It is also the one that turns into a
question worth asking in supervision, which is the difference between a
supervision hour that counts and one that is merely logged.
