# The Ceiling

*Working note, 20 September 2026. The companion to `the-floor.md`. The floor is the minimum rate of accumulation. This is what the rate is for. The counselling-side equivalent of Studio Day's `the-ceiling.md`, and it differs from it in one structural way: this practice has **two** ceilings, in sequence, with a narrow hinge between them. Sourced in `verification.md`.*

---

## The position, stated once

**By 2034 the practice should be able to produce a counsellor at UBC or SFU who is hired for the thing nobody else in the room can do — hold the access lens and the mood lens at once — and a record that would justify the years whether or not that post is ever taken.**

Both halves are load-bearing, for the same reason they are in the studio. A file assembled in the year it is needed reads as assembly. A file that accumulated for eight years reads as a life.

## Ceiling two · The hiring panel, ~2034

Also verified, and unlike the studio's ceiling it is a public document that can be read today. A UBC **Wellness Advisor** post in Counselling Services, AAPS Management & Professional, non-union, **$7,283.75–$10,474.08 monthly**, listed a master's in counselling psychology, RCC registration and three years' experience — all as **preferred**, with "equivalent combination of education and experience" accepted.

Its core duty: *"determining appropriate care levels within a stepped care model."* Its first-listed preference: *"triage experience in fast-paced mental health settings."* Then crisis intervention, wellness programming, cross-cultural competence, flexible scheduling, and expertise with **international, Aboriginal, or graduate students**.

SFU publishes named counselling pathways for Indigenous, 2SLGBTQIA+, Black, **disabled and neurodivergent**, and graduate students.

**The finding is not that the governing question fits the job. The governing question is the job description.** Who campus stepped care leaves out is the daily operational problem of both services, and the disability pathway is already staffed at SFU and short of people.

Two honest qualifications, both from the verified roster:

- **The master's is the floor, not the differentiator.** All ~40 clinicians at UBC Counselling Services hold one; registration is near-universal. What distinguishes candidates is triage fluency in a stepped-care service, population-specific expertise, group facilitation, and crisis capability.
- **The leadership tier is almost entirely PhD, EdD or R.Psych.** The master's gets the room. The doctorate gets the service. That is the honest answer to what a doctorate confers in BC beyond RCC, and it closes item 3 of the verification list in `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` more usefully than a title question would have.

## The hinge · A practicum seat, January 2033

Between the two ceilings sits the narrowest point in the whole sequence, and it is not the degree.

- UBC Counselling Services takes **four** master's practicum students a year. Deadline **15 January**. Requires a **20–30 minute recording of a mock counselling session**, two references, transcripts, and virtual interviews for the shortlist. 600 hours, minimum 180 direct, 2.5–3 days a week, mid-August to end of April, with two hours of individual supervision weekly.
- SFU's equivalent takes students **from any institution**, wants a 25–30 minute mock video, and runs two streams closing 30 November and 25 January.
- At SFU, **students secure their own placements**, in Year 2, a full year ahead.
- Training inside the service is the documented route to being known by the people who later hire.

So the target is not a post in 2034. It is **a seat in January 2033**, and everything in the relational ledger is aimed at it. Two consequences that have to be planned rather than discovered:

1. **The practicum year is not compatible with full-time work.** 2.5–3 days a week on placement, for eight months. The income question is a 2032 planning problem at the latest, and it belongs in the November review from 2030 onward.
2. **The mock session recording is a real artefact with a real standard.** The fortnightly spoken piece is its rehearsal, starting now, seven years early, at no cost.

## What the exceptional file is made of

The part of a counselling psychology file that straight-through applicants cannot fill is the standing: years of supervised service, a published piece of research, an employer inside the institution, and a question already formed. Those are exactly the things a career produces by default, provided they are documented as they happen.

Three things would make this file exceptional rather than merely complete:

1. **A published scoping review, before the application.** Not a research assistantship line — an authored, citable object that names who campus provision leaves out. Almost no master's applicant arrives with one, and it *is* the governing question, answered as far as the literature allows.
2. **Dual standing, again.** A counsellor with a decade-long exhibiting practice and disability studies scholarship can make claims about mood, access and attention that neither a clinician nor an artist alone can make, and be believed on both. This is the argument for the research question, not a biography.
3. **The disability lens, held from inside the population, with a degree behind it.** The verified faculty map says nobody at UBC or SFU is asking this question. That is the opportunity and the burden: the review exists because it was made, and the standpoint is the reason the question is well formed rather than a disclosure to be managed in a statement.

## Asymmetric moves, cheapest now

**Target the institution, not only the role.** UBC's waiver gives most staff groups 12 undergraduate credits per 12-month period, and Management & Professional groups 12 **graduate** credits. SFU's AD 9.10 covers graduate programmes more broadly. **The prerequisites are undergraduate courses**, so a staff post at either university — in any group — funds the ladder. The Accessibility Advisor role that Labs aims at for 2029–31 would, if it lands, pay for prerequisites due 31 December 2030. Tight, but real.

**The loop worth seeing whole.** At UBC, the Wellness Advisor role *is* AAPS — the group that also gets graduate credits. AAPS post → graduate waiver → part-funded master's → practicum inside the service → counsellor post. Each step is documented and each makes the next one easier.

**Be in the room where the standards are written.** CHCPBC is consulting on bylaws this autumn and states that entry-to-practice standards for psychotherapy *"have not yet been established."* The webinar is **14 October 2026**. Nothing else available this year costs less or returns more.

**Write the question down annually.** One paragraph, revised each November. Eight dated versions are a research trajectory. Cheapest item in the system and every door reads it. See `the-question.md`.

## How this relates to the floor

The floor is the minimum rate at which the five ledgers keep accumulating through a degree year, a job change or a practicum. The ceiling is what they accumulate toward.

The test, asked once a year on the first Wednesday of November: **did each of the five ledgers gain at least one entry?** A year where all five moved is a good year, whatever it felt like. Nothing here requires a heroic month, and this profession punishes heroic months specifically.
