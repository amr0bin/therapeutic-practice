# Part one · What Therapeutic Practice is

## The name, and what it is not

**Studio Day is to Andrea Robin Studio what Therapeutic Practice is to Therapeutic Studio.** The system is not the property.

- **Therapeutic Studio** stays the house — the public name, the notebook, the question, and on registration the clinical practice. Settled 13 September 2026 in `06-the-naming-and-the-room.md`. Nothing here reopens it.
- **Therapeutic Practice** is the private operating system that runs the property and the two tracks that sit outside it: eligibility for the master's, and eligibility for the job. It is a standing order, five ledgers, a record and a calendar. It is never a public-facing name, because "practice" in this field means a clinical practice and there isn't one yet.

## The three things it has to make into one thing

Right now there are three separate efforts and no system joining them. Each was read on 20 September:

| What exists | State | What it is actually for |
|---|---|---|
| **therapeuticstudio.ca** | Live. Creative health studio; Make Space as a year-long open inquiry; Other Bodies (52 cards); weekly sessions via ohHEY with Shauna Kaendo; YouTube practices; Substack | The public register. The notebook a supervisor reads before meeting you |
| **make-space-curriculum** | 5 files, 31 commits, **no README, no licence** | The resource ledger — currently illegible and, in copyright terms, closed |
| **career** | 10 files, 74 commits, **no README, no licence** | The standing track — the hidden ladder, the sequence, twenty years |

Three repos with no README are three repos nobody can read, including a future supervisor, a hiring panel, or you in 2030. **No licence means all rights reserved** — the exact opposite of the stated intention to share openly. That is a two-hour fix and it is the cheapest item in this entire document.

The system's first job is to make these three one record with one spine and one calendar. Not one repo — the audiences differ, and `00-portfolio-spine-shared.md` is right that a property trying to be two things is two half-things. One *system*, reading across three surfaces.

# Part two · The two ceilings, and the hinge between them

Studio Day has one ceiling in 2036. Therapeutic Practice has two, in sequence, and the corpus has been planning toward the wrong one.

## Ceiling one — the admissions panel, 1 December 2030

Named, dated and verified. Both UBC and SFU close on **1 December** for the following September. That is the date the whole file points at.

## Ceiling two — the hiring panel, ~2034

Also verified, and it is a public document you can read today. A UBC **Wellness Advisor** post in Counselling Services, AAPS M&P, non-union, **$7,283.75–$10,474.08 monthly**, listed:

- Master's in Counselling Psychology *preferred*, RCC *preferred*, three years' experience *preferred* — all preferred, with "equivalent combination of education and experience" accepted
- Core duty: **"determining appropriate care levels within a stepped care model"**
- First-listed preference: **"triage experience in fast-paced mental health settings"**
- Named preferences: expertise with **international, Aboriginal, or graduate students**; crisis intervention; wellness programming facilitation; flexible scheduling

Read that against the governing question — *who does campus stepped care for depression leave out* — and the finding is not that the question is a good fit for the job. The finding is that **the question is the job description**. SFU goes further: its counselling service publishes named pathways for Indigenous, 2SLGBTQIA+, Black, **disabled and neurodivergent**, and graduate students. The specialism this portfolio is building is not a niche to be argued for at interview. It is a staffed pathway that already exists and is short of people.

Two honest qualifications. The master's is the floor, not the differentiator — every clinician on UBC's ~40-person roster holds one, and registration is near-universal. And the leadership tier at UBC Counselling Services is almost entirely PhD/EdD/R.Psych. The master's gets the room; the doctorate gets the service. That is the real answer to what a doctorate confers, and it closes item 3 of the verification list in `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` more usefully than a title question would have.

## The hinge, which is neither of those — a practicum seat, ~2033

This is the most important structural finding in this document.

- UBC Counselling Services takes **four** master's practicum students a year. Applications close **15 January**, require a **20–30 minute recording of a mock counselling session**, two references, and virtual interviews for the shortlist. 600 hours, minimum 180 direct service hours, 2.5–3 days a week, mid-August to end of April.
- SFU's equivalent takes students **from any institution**, requires a 25–30 minute mock session video, and runs two streams with deadlines of 30 November and 25 January.
- At SFU, **students secure their own placements**, a full year ahead, in Year 2 of the programme.
- Training inside the service is the documented route to being known by the people who later hire.

So the thing to build toward is not "a counsellor post in 2034." It is **one of four seats at UBC Counselling Services, or a self-secured seat at SFU, applied for in January 2033**. That is earlier, far more precise, and it changes what the relational ledger is for. It also means the practicum-year income problem has to be planned now, not discovered in 2032: 2.5–3 days a week on placement is not compatible with a full-time role, and no version of this is.

The corpus treats the MA as the gate and the job as the prize. The practicum is the hinge, and it is the narrowest point in the entire sequence.

# Part three · What verification changed

Nine items. Four reverse something the corpus currently states. Full sources in the appendix.

### 1 · The GRE sits on the thesis route — the one already committed to

`-the-approach.md` says: *"a third-party site says GRE; the department pages do not — check the department, not the aggregator."* **That is backwards.** UBC's own Graduate & Postdoctoral Studies MA page says verbatim *"The GRE is required by all applicants,"* and the Academic Calendar says MA applicants *"must write the Graduate Record Examination (GRE) general test."* The MEd page and the PhD page both say it is not required.

The conflict is internal to UBC, not between UBC and an aggregator. And it lands precisely on the **MA (thesis)** — the route `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` has already committed to as non-negotiable. A general test costs money and months of preparation, and on the current runway it falls in 2029–30, the same stretch as finishing Leeds, the advisor role, submitting the review and completing prerequisites.

Many counselling programmes dropped the GRE after 2020 without updating central pages, and the department page — which could not be read from here — is where a rescission would appear first. **This is the single highest-value question to ask ECPS, and it can be asked this month by email.** Until answered, assume the GRE is required and put it in the 2029 calendar.

### 2 · The MEd genuinely does close the doctoral door — now verified, at UBC

The Academic Calendar states PhD applicants need *"a thesis-based master's degree equivalent to the CNPS M.A. in counselling"* with an 80% average. The standing decision in `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` was correct and is now sourced rather than assumed.

Two things it did not anticipate:

- **At SFU the thesis route is a third thing** — neither elective nor a separate admission stream, but a **gate passed after entry**. SFU's page says students *"Complete an MEd or an MA (with faculty approval)."* The MA is 52 units with an 18-unit thesis; the MEd is 44 units with a comprehensive exam. So at SFU, entering does not secure the thesis route. Faculty approval does, and that has to be solved in Year 1.
- **SFU has no counselling psychology doctorate at all** — only a PhD in Education. If a counselling psychology doctorate is the destination, **UBC is the only door in BC**, and that materially changes how the two programmes compare.

### 3 · SFU's prerequisites are different from UBC's, and larger

The corpus has only ever held UBC's list. SFU requires **six** undergraduate courses completed **before applying**:

1. Cognitive psychology **or** educational psychology
2. An undergraduate course in **counselling skills** (SFU names EDUC 423)
3. An undergraduate course in **counselling theories** (SFU names EDUC 323)
4. Developmental psychology
5. Research methods
6. One further: social psychology, multicultural counselling, personality theory, psychopathology, abnormal psychology, sociology, cultural anthropology, or women's studies

UBC's Calendar list is senior courses in **learning**, **statistics**, and **either abnormal psychology or behaviour disorders**, plus **CNPS 362** (Basic Interviewing Skills) and **CNPS 365** (Introduction to Theories of Counselling).

**The ladder must cover the union of both lists if both doors stay open**, and the union is roughly eight courses, not five. Note that items 2 and 3 at SFU and CNPS 362/365 at UBC are the same kind of thing — counselling skills and counselling theories — so they may be satisfiable once each if the equivalency tables allow. They may also not be. This is the question that most changes the timeline and it is answerable by two emails.

UBC publishes an *"Approved M.A. & M.Ed. Prerequisites"* equivalency page, confirmed to exist but unreadable from here. Get it.

### 4 · The helping-experience requirement is a stated minimum, not a soft preference

`-the-approach.md` records *"Relevant experience — no minimum, but considered."* UBC's Calendar states **"a minimum of three years work/volunteer experience that involves helping relationships."** SFU states **"approximately two years (or 400 hours)."**

Three years is a hard clock. Crisis Centre BC's next training cohort is **January 2027**; applications are currently closed and reopen for it. January 2027 to December 2030 is just under four years — it works, with one year of slack. **If the January 2027 intake is missed, the slack is gone.** Make Space facilitation, the ohHEY sessions and Slow Leaf plausibly already count toward this and should be inventoried and dated rather than assumed not to count.

### 5 · The regulated title is not what the corpus assumes, and the standards do not exist yet

Verified: HPOA came into force 1 April 2026; **counselling therapists are regulated from 29 November 2027**; applications to the college open summer 2027. The corpus's date is right.

But BC is regulating **psychotherapy**, and the reserved title has **not been prescribed** — CCPA and BCACC jointly proposed "Clinical Counsellor" or "Psychotherapist" in 2023 and the Ministry has not chosen. CHCPBC states plainly that *"the profession-specific eligibility standards for psychotherapy have not yet been established,"* and CCPA states that **"no designations will be automatically ported."**

Three consequences:

- Anyone graduating around 2034 registers under CHCPBC's *regular route for recent graduates* — a route whose requirements are unwritten. The hedge is to clear the most demanding published standard that currently exists (below).
- **CHCPBC is consulting on bylaws this autumn, with a webinar on 14 October 2026.** That is free, dated, and it puts you in the room where the standards you will be held to are being set. It is a relational and standing move as much as an information one, and nothing else on the calendar this year costs less.
- The house rule against ever using *therapist* or *psychotherapist* is correctly anticipating the regulation. **Verify separately** whether a property named *Therapeutic* Studio attracts advertising-standards attention once the title is protected. Not a reason to reopen the naming decision; a reason to have the answer before November 2027.

### 6 · RCC and CCC measure different things, and two courses are the usual failure point

BCACC's RCC requires a master's covering **six core areas**: developmental psychology or psychopathology; counselling or personality theories; **group therapy theory and practice**; **family therapy theory and practice**; basic research design; professional ethics. Plus **100 hours of clinical supervision, at least 25 of them direct**. Published guidance states BCACC *"does not require you to report client contact hours, only clinical supervision hours."*

CCPA's CCC is the mirror image: **150 direct client hours** in supervised practicum but only **10 hours** of direct supervision.

The operational consequence is small, cheap and easy to miss: **group therapy and family therapy are the two areas people most often fail to have on the transcript**, and they are usually elective choices made inside the programme in 2032 for reasons that have nothing to do with registration. Write them into the plan now.

Also: **UBC is CACEP-accredited and SFU is not.** Accredited graduates are fast-tracked to CCC; everyone else submits detailed course descriptions. UBC's accreditation is listed as expiring **30 September 2026** — ten days from now — with a re-application filed in August 2025. Presumably routine, worth confirming.

### 7 · The staff tuition waiver turns the prerequisite ladder from a cost into a benefit

**UBC:** 12 undergraduate credits or dollar equivalent per 12-month period for most staff groups; **graduate** credits only for Management & Professional (AAPS) and above. Three months' service required. Application and student fees excluded.

**SFU:** Policy AD 9.10 covers Senate-approved credit courses **including graduate programs**, more broadly than UBC. Submit no earlier than week 6 of the previous term and no later than the tuition deadline in week 2.

**The prerequisites are undergraduate courses.** So a staff role at either university — in any group, not just AAPS — covers the ladder. This interlocks the two properties more tightly than `the-studio-day-pattern.md` realised: the Accessibility Advisor post that Labs is aimed at for 2029–31 would, if it lands, fund the prerequisites that are due by 31 December 2030. Tight, but real, and it is another argument for ring one in `08-the-target-map.md`.

At UBC specifically, the Wellness Advisor role *is* AAPS — the group that also gets graduate credits. That is a genuine loop worth seeing whole: AAPS role → graduate waiver → part-funded master's → practicum inside the service → counsellor post.

### 8 · The mock-session recording is real; the admissions video assessment is not confirmed

`-the-approach.md` states as fact: *"Video assessment for shortlisted applicants, from 2027 start."* That could not be verified — the department page was unreadable, and no accessible UBC page mentions it. **SFU's interview is confirmed**, with contact roughly 8–12 weeks after the deadline.

What *is* confirmed is a **20–30 minute recorded mock counselling session** required for practicum applications at UBC, and 25–30 minutes at SFU. So the fortnightly spoken piece keeps its justification — just aimed at a different and verified target, two years later than assumed. Downgrade the admissions video from fact to **verify**.

### 9 · The timeline runs a year longer than the corpus states

SFU's minimum is **eight terms** — "two calendar years plus two terms" — with the MA thesis permitted up to nine. A September 2031 entry therefore finishes around **spring 2034**, not 2033, with the Year 3 practicum falling in 2033–34. Stage three in `00-portfolio-spine-shared.md` closes on a condition rather than a year, so nothing breaks; but the intensity table in `the-studio-day-pattern.md` should read **2031–34** for the MA and **2034–36** for registration and practice.

# Part four · The floor

What survives the hardest year. Two hard years are already visible: 2027–29 (Leeds part-time plus a full-time role) and 2033–34 (the practicum year).

**The floor, in all seasons:**

1. **One notebook entry a month.** Not weekly. The weekly cadence in `-the-approach.md` is a full-intensity figure and will break during Leeds; a floor that breaks is not a floor. Monthly is defensible across a degree year and still produces 48 dated entries by the 2030 panel.
2. **The hours log updated within 24 hours of any service.** Non-negotiable, because it is the only record that cannot be reconstructed.
3. **The crisis-line shift kept.** The service hours are the three-year clock.
4. **One calendar check a month** against the dated list in Part seven.

That is the whole floor. Everything else — the review, the faculty map, the spoken piece, the resource documentation — is full-intensity work and is allowed to stop.

**The floor during the practicum year, 2033–34, is smaller still:** the hours log and nothing else. The placement *is* the practice that year.

| **Shown** | The notebook; the fortnightly spoken piece; the conference presentation; practitioner interviews published with credit. Never anything client-derived, at any stage, de-identified or not | *Did something go out with a date on it?* |

# Part eight · The named reader, and the gap

Studio Day recruits a critic. Therapeutic Practice eventually gets a clinical supervisor, and that relationship is a professional requirement with its own rules rather than something to arrange.

**The honest problem: between now and 2031 there is no reader.** A supervisor requires someone to supervise, and there is deliberately no clinical work. That is roughly five years in which the only person assessing the work is the person doing it — the exact failure mode the whole pattern exists to prevent, and this property's weakest joint.

Three partial answers, in order of how soon they can exist:

1. **The crisis-line supervisor**, from 2027. Institutional supervision of real service, with a reference at the end. Not a reader of the thinking, but a reader of the practice, and the first external assessment available.
2. **The lab or RA supervisor**, from 2028. The person who reads the review before it is public. Closest to Studio Day's critic, and the strongest reference the file will carry.
3. **The practitioner interviewed twice a year**, already in `-the-approach.md`. Make one of them a standing reader rather than only an interviewee — someone who will read the review draft and say whether it is naive about the room. An RCC with ten years in a university counselling service is the ideal person, and there is no reason that relationship cannot start in 2027.

Name a person for each slot and write the date they were asked in `references.md`. An unnamed reader is not a reader.

# Part ten · What excellence is in this field

The user of this document asked what excellence could look like here. It is worth being precise, because the obvious answer — more training, more modalities, more hours — is close to wrong.

**The uncomfortable finding from the field's own research: therapist outcomes do not reliably improve with years of experience.** Experience produces confidence far more reliably than it produces competence. What does improve outcomes is measurement and deliberate practice — routine outcome monitoring session by session, and structured work on specific skills with a coach who watches you do them. Most practitioners never build that loop. Excellence begins with deciding to be someone who does.

That maps exactly onto the system already described. The hours log is the beginning of an outcome record. The named reader is the beginning of a coach. Neither is a counselling idea; both are the same idea Studio Day already runs on.

Four layers, then:

**One — excellence as a practitioner is measured, not felt.** Know your own outcome data. Learn rupture repair specifically: the alliance is the most robust predictor of outcome, therapists differ substantially in their contribution to it, and the skill that separates them is repairing ruptures rather than avoiding them. Prefer cultural humility, which is a stance, over cultural competence, which pretends to be a body of knowledge.

**Two — excellence as a scholar-practitioner is asking a question the field has not asked, from inside the population.** The verified faculty map says nobody at UBC or SFU is asking this one. That is the opportunity and the burden: the review will be the thing that exists because you made it, and the standpoint is not a disclosure to be managed in a statement but the reason the question is well formed.

**Three — excellence in the institution is changing what the service offers, not only staffing a chair in it.** Both universities run stepped care; both publish population-specific pathways; both are visibly short of people who can design provision as well as deliver it. The verified evidence that the leadership tier holds doctorates is the honest shape of that ambition, and it is the first real argument this portfolio has produced *for* the counselling doctoral route rather than merely keeping it open.

**Four — excellence is lasting.** The modal failure in this profession is attrition, and the second is the quiet erosion of the practitioner into someone who has stopped noticing. The Studio Day rule — accumulation, not intensity — is not a productivity preference here. It is the clinical version of the same thing: a year in which all five ledgers gained one small entry and you are still recognisably yourself is a year on the trajectory.

And the specific form of excellence available to this practice and to almost nobody else: **being credible in the studio and the clinic at once.** A registered counsellor with a decade of exhibited practice, disability studies scholarship, and accessibility work inside a university is a combination BC currently does not have. The sequencing in `the-studio-day-pattern.md` is what makes it reachable. This document's job is to make sure the counselling half is built to the same standard as the painting.

# Part eleven · What must not happen

Carried from the corpus, plus what verification added.

- **The January 2027 Crisis Centre cohort missed.** It is the three-year clock and the first supervisor.
- **A participant, a consent sheet, a pre/post measure or instrument development** before an ethics board and a supervisor exist. Unchanged and absolute.
- **Any identifiable participant material on any public surface**, at any stage.
- **The peer register and a clinical offer on the same page.**
- **The hours log lapsing.** Everything else can be done late.
- **An MEd entered by default**, or an SFU MA assumed rather than secured with faculty approval in Year 1.
- **Group therapy and family therapy missed** in elective choices inside the programme.
- **The practicum year discovered rather than planned**, financially.
- **The prevalence claim published without a citation.** Still outstanding, still one afternoon's work, still blocking every public statement of the question.
- **AI used on the application itself.** This system prepares the record, the evidence and the calendar. The statement, the answers and the video are yours alone.
