# The five ledgers

*Working note, 20 September 2026. The measure that replaces feeling. Translated from Studio Day's five accounts into what registration and hiring actually count. Reviewed once a year, on a fixed date.*

**The review date: the first Wednesday of November.** It sits a month before the 1 December application deadline in every year that has one, which means the review is done while there is still time to act on it.

**The test, asked once:** did each of the five ledgers gain at least one entry this year?

A year where all five moved is a good year, whatever it felt like. A year where three moved is still a year on the trajectory. A year where none moved is the failure this whole system exists to make visible — and it is invisible without this file, because it feels identical to a busy year.

---

## The five

### 1 · Corpus — hours and competencies

**What it holds.** Crisis-line service hours. Training hours. Supervision hours banked toward 100/25. Prerequisite courses passed. Certificates: ASIST, safeTALK, Mental Health First Aid. Later: practicum hours, direct client hours, modalities trained in, registration.

**Why it is different from every other property's corpus.** Studio Day's corpus is a catalogue anyone can be shown. Accessible Learning Labs' is a library anyone can download. **Clinical work can never be either.** Confidentiality is the condition of the work, and no amount of de-identification turns a person's material into a public record.

So this ledger is **private and externally attested** — by a supervisor, an institution and eventually a registrar, rather than by a repository and a DOI. Different mechanism, same job: verification by someone who is not you.

**Lives in:** `hours-log.csv` and `supervision-log.csv`, private repository.

**November question:** *did the hours log gain entries a registrar would accept?*

---

### 2 · Shown — the public record

**What it holds.** The notebook. The fortnightly spoken piece. Practitioner interviews, published with credit. The conference presentation. Workshops and psychoeducation. The Make Space retrospective. Documented and licensed resources.

**The absolute limit:** never anything client-derived, at any stage, de-identified or not. `house-rules.md` governs.

**Lives in:** therapeuticstudio.ca, the Substack, YouTube, and the resource repositories.

**November question:** *did something go out with a date on it?*

---

### 3 · Funded — money moving toward the work

**What it holds.** Not fee income for years — it cannot be, pre-registration, and attempting it breaches `house-rules.md`. So the pre-licence form of this ledger is money moving *toward* the work: waiver-funded prerequisites, funded training, bursaries, an RA stipend, conference travel support. After registration it becomes what it is everywhere else: fee income at a professional rate.

**Why keep it at all when nothing can be charged?** Because Studio Day's rule — *nothing free that should be paid* — has a pre-licence version: **nothing absorbed that should be funded.** Paying for your own prerequisites out of salary when a staff waiver would cover them is the same error as painting a commission for free, and it compounds over eight years.

**November question:** *did someone else pay for part of this?*

---

### 4 · Scholarly — the research record

**What it holds.** The registered scoping review, stage by stage: protocol on OSF → screening → extraction → synthesis → preprint → journal → conference. Later the MA thesis, and whatever follows it.

**The one ledger that behaves like the others,** because research is public by design. It is also the ledger the MA explicitly adjudicates on, and the only one the studio can close on its own without an institution's permission.

**November question:** *did the review move one stage?*

Six stages, four years. One stage a year is behind. Two is on pace.

---

### 5 · Relational — the people

**What it holds.** The three referees and the date each was asked. The crisis-line supervisor. The lab supervisor. Practitioners interviewed. **Named people inside UBC and SFU counselling services, because they decide the practicum seat.** The standing reader. CHCPBC consultation participation.

**This is the ledger that changed most in translation.** In Studio Day it is a network kept warm. Here it is the mechanism by which the hinge in `the-ceiling.md` opens — four practicum seats at UBC, self-secured placements at SFU, and training inside the service as the documented route to being hired by it.

**It is also the only ledger that cannot be produced in the year it is needed**, and the one most applicants are thinnest in.

**Kept as:** a list of names and dates in `references.md`, private. Not a feeling about a field.

**November question:** *did a named person who can open a door learn your name this year?*

**Annual rate:** two new named relationships, each kept warm with two contacts a year.

---

## The review, in order

First Wednesday of November. Ninety minutes.

1. **Close the logs.** Everything from the year recorded before anything is assessed.
2. **Five questions, above.** Write the answer, not a score. One line each.
3. **Read `the-ceiling.md`** and ask whether the two ceilings and the hinge are still the right targets. They will change; the file is revised, not obeyed.
4. **Read `verification.md`** and strike what has been answered. Add what the year raised.
5. **Revise `the-question.md`** — one paragraph, under two hundred words, dated, appended below last year's and never overwritten.
6. **Read `the-floor.md`** against next year's shape. If a hard stretch is coming, say so now and drop to the floor deliberately.

Then close the file until November.
