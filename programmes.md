# The programmes

*Working note, 20 September 2026. UBC and SFU side by side, from primary sources, checked 20 September 2026. Everything here is sourced in `verification.md`; anything not confirmed is marked **verify** and must not be planned around. Supersedes the programme facts in `therapeutic-studio-the-approach.md`.*

**Why this file exists:** the two programmes are not variants of one thing. They differ on the thesis route, the prerequisites, the doctoral door, accreditation and the shape of the practicum year — and each of those differences moves a date or closes a route. Deciding between them in 2030 without this file written would mean deciding on reputation.

## The four differences that actually decide it

### 1 · The doctoral door is at UBC only

UBC's Academic Calendar states PhD applicants need *"a thesis-based master's degree equivalent to the CNPS M.A. in counselling."* Explicit, and it confirms what `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` had committed to on reasoning alone.

SFU has **no counselling psychology doctorate at all** — only a PhD in Education. So if a counselling psychology doctorate stays live as a route, **UBC is the only door in British Columbia**, and that outranks most of the other rows in the table.

### 2 · SFU's thesis route is not secured by admission

SFU's page says students *"Complete an MEd or an MA (with faculty approval)."* That is a third thing — neither elective nor a separate stream, but a gate passed **after** entry, with a supervisor's agreement.

If SFU is chosen and the thesis matters, **faculty approval is a Year 1 problem**, and the supervisor conversation has to happen before enrolment, not after. `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` warns against "an MEd taken by default because it was easier to enter." At SFU the risk is subtler: an MA intended, and not approved.

### 3 · The GRE sits on UBC's thesis route

UBC's Graduate & Postdoctoral Studies MA page states *"The GRE is required by all applicants."* The Academic Calendar agrees. The MEd page and the PhD page both say it is not required.

So the route already committed to for doctoral reasons is the one route in either university carrying an admissions test. That is money and months, landing in 2029 alongside finishing Leeds.

> **The department's own pages could not be read** — `ecps.educ.ubc.ca` sits behind a challenge page. Many counselling programmes dropped the GRE after 2020 without updating central pages, and the department site is where a rescission would appear first. **This is the single highest-value question to ask, and it is one email.** Until answered: assume required, and budget for it.

### 4 · CACEP changes how CCC is obtained, not whether

UBC's MA and MEd are CACEP-accredited; SFU's programme is not. Accredited graduates are fast-tracked to Canadian Certified Counsellor; everyone else submits detailed course descriptions. CCC remains available either way — it is a difference of process, not eligibility.

UBC's accreditation is listed as expiring **30 September 2026** with a re-application filed 28 August 2025. Presumably routine. **Verify.**

## Where SFU is genuinely stronger

The table above reads as an argument for UBC. Two things cut the other way and both matter to this particular question.

**The faculty cluster.** Sharalyn Jordan, Emily Cameron and Sharon Hou form an unusually dense community-engaged and participatory group, and Jordan specifically *"partners with community agencies to evaluate counselling practices and programs."* That is the methodological home of this practice's question. And **Elina Birmingham** runs the **Autism Mentorship Initiative** improving campus experience for autistic SFU students — disability, inside a university, as student experience. Read against SFU's published disabled and neurodivergent counselling pathway, that is the tightest institutional fit for the governing question anywhere in the two universities.

**The shape of the years.** Evening classes, explicitly designed for working professionals. Against a full-time role and a portfolio of three other properties, that is not a minor convenience.

The cost is the Year 3 practicum at 2–3 days a week, self-secured — which is not compatible with full-time work regardless of when classes run. Both programmes hit the same wall in the practicum year; SFU is easier in the two years before it.

## Decide when, and on what

**Not now.** The decision belongs in 2029–30, with the faculty map at its second pass and the supervisor conversations had. What is decided now is only this: **both doors stay open**, which means the prerequisite ladder covers the union of both lists (`prerequisites.md`), and the GRE is budgeted for.

The three questions whose answers will actually decide it:

1. **Is a counselling psychology doctorate still live?** If yes, UBC, and the argument is over. Reread `05-the-two-doctoral-routes.md` at each decision point.
2. **Does a supervisor want the question?** A named person who wants this work outranks every row in the table above. That is what `faculty-map.md` is being built for, from now rather than from 2029.
3. **Is the GRE real?** If UBC still requires it for the MA, and SFU does not require it at all, that is a genuine cost difference on the one route that matters.

## Also verified, and useful

**Staff tuition waivers.** UBC: 12 undergraduate credits per 12-month period for most staff groups; **12 graduate credits for Management & Professional (AAPS) and above**; three months' service required; application and student fees excluded. SFU: policy AD 9.10 covers Senate-approved credit courses **including graduate programmes**, more broadly than UBC; submit no earlier than week 6 of the previous term and no later than the tuition deadline in week 2.

**The prerequisites are undergraduate courses**, so a staff post at either university in *any* group pays for the ladder. See `the-ceiling.md` → *Asymmetric moves*.
